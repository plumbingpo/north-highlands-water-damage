Every water damage restoration job starts with extraction, and no other early step matters more. Structural materials, floors, possessions and building systems all take less damage when standing water comes out sooner. With each hour it sits, water works farther in: through carpet to the pad, up the drywall, into wood framing and down to the subfloor. Across North Highlands, North Highlands Restoration handles emergency extraction with industrial portable and truck-mounted machines that can remove hundreds of gallons a minute.

Our truck-mounted extraction units are among the most powerful water removal systems available, with the suction to pull water from carpets, hard floors and structural materials far more effectively than consumer or rental equipment. Confined spaces, wall cavities and the gaps between floor layers call for different gear, so we use portable extractors, specialty tools and submersible pumps in places the truck-mounted hoses cannot get to.

Removing visible water is only part of what professional extraction involves. Water migrates behind baseboards, below flooring, into wall cavities and under cabinets, and our technicians, who are IICRC-certified, locate it with moisture detection tools. The more moisture our specialty tools take from these concealed areas ahead of drying, the shorter and lighter the structural drying job becomes, and the lower the chance of secondary mold growth.

What a Professional Brings to Water Extraction

The speed and thoroughness of water extraction directly determine the extent of damage to your property and the cost of restoration. Truck-mounted extractors move water at hundreds of times the rate of consumer tools, and a job that could cost you a full day with a shop vac is done by our machines in minutes. Speed matters for more than comfort: every extra hour of water sitting against building materials can mean thousands of dollars more in damage.

Professional extraction also addresses moisture that you cannot see or reach with consumer tools. It follows plumbing lines and electrical conduits, slips between flooring layers, climbs drywall by wicking and is drawn into carpet pad through capillary action. Surface extraction by itself leaves much of this behind, so our team uses specialty techniques and tools aimed at the hidden moisture. Being this thorough shortens drying, cuts equipment costs and, above all, helps stop trapped moisture from turning into hidden mold.

Maintenance Tips for Water Extraction

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Shelving in low areas keeps belongings out of the water if the floor floods
  • Install automatic water shutoff valves that detect leaks and stop the flow
  • Add our number, (754) 342-6499, to your cell so help is one tap away in a water emergency
  • Look beneath sinks, behind toilets and beside appliances every so often to catch leaks early
  • Place leak detection sensors in the key risk areas and test that they work
  • A sump pump that has not been checked may fail in a storm, so test it ahead of wet weather

Warning Signs for Water Extraction

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drips or a steady stream coming out of a ceiling, a wall or a fixture
  • Floors flooded by a fire sprinkler or suppression system going off
  • Standing water on any floor surface, whatever the source
  • Storm water collecting in the lowest level of the home
  • Water visible in crawl spaces, mechanical rooms or utility areas
  • Ongoing leakage from the plumbing, the air conditioning or an appliance
  • Pooled water on the carpet, or a soggy feel with every step
